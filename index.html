Abstract Modern local subnet architectures exhibit non-deterministic packet propagation characteristics when transitioning between asymmetric peer transceivers. This paper presents an empirical analysis of multi-tenant distributed signal graphs under rigorous latency bounds, quantifying throughput variations within isolated routing clusters. Experimental data collected over high-density academic intranet infrastructure demonstrates a 23.4% reduction in jitter when employing autonomous packet serialization strategies.

I. Introduction & System Topology

The escalating throughput mandates of client-orchestrated edge clusters demand resilient peer dispatch paradigms capable of operating over heterogeneous layer-2 local area infrastructure. Standard dynamic signaling frameworks introduce non-trivial round-trip latency during initial cryptographic session negotiations, compounding connection starvation under restrictive firewall matrices.

To address these structural bottlenecks, we propose an autonomous local-first topology wherein transient states are prioritized across physical subnet boundaries. By decoupling ephemeral media transport from centralized persistence coordinators, our model achieves continuous deterministic signal broadcast with microsecond resolution.
